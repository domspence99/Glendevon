Welcome to Glendevon

A genuine piece of Staithes history

Glendevon Cottage is a delightful former fisherman's dwelling facing onto the elegant Barrass Square, in the very heart of the ancient fishing village of Staithes. With documentary records stretching back over 170 years, this is a place that has sheltered mariners, miners, and their families across generations.

Inside, you'll find an open fire in the lounge, exposed wooden beams, folding shutters, and wainscotting — the bones of a working seafarer's home, now thoughtfully furnished for guests. The cottage sleeps up to four, welcomes dogs, and sits a two-minute stroll from the beach.

Est. Early 19th Century

Fishermen, mariners & a life lived by the sea

Glendevon's documentary record stretches back at least 170 years. Families working in seafaring and mining — industries vital to Staithes — made this cottage their home for generations. Local names of Ward, Shippey, Crooks and Theaker are woven through its story.

In 1881, young Matthew Ward was recorded at sea aboard the True Love, a Whitby fishing vessel crewed entirely by Staithes men. By 1911 he had turned to fish hawking, delivering orders by horse and cart to local villages — and one can easily imagine him calling his wares on The Staithe.
