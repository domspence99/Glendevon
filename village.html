North Yorkshire

The Village of Staithes

A Landing Place

Ancient harbour, living history

The name Staithes means 'landing place' — and it's been a landing place for fishermen, artists, and adventurers for centuries. Thought to have originated as the port for Seaton Hall and the village of Hinderwell, its sheltered harbour once held 80 full-time fishing boats. Today a handful still operate, keeping the old traditions alive.

The village is famous for its 'coble' — an ancient fishing vessel reputedly modelled on the Viking longboat. Many cottages here are named after former fishing boats; there's even one called 'True Love', the same vessel on which Glendevon's former resident Matthew Ward served in 1881.

Staithes' tightly packed cottages, ancient alleyways and courtyards have made it a favourite for filmmakers — Daniel Day-Lewis's Oscar-winning film Phantom Thread (2017) filmed scenes here, as did the children's BBC series Old Jack's Boat.

Famous Resident

James Cook apprenticed here as a young man before his voyages of discovery.

The Staithes Group

In the 1890s, 'Northern Impressionist' artists including Dame Laura Knight made the village their home.

Food & Drink

The Cod and Lobster and The Royal George serve meals, ales, wines and spirits in the village.

Shops

Butcher, deli, toy shop, 2 art galleries, gift shop, cafes, museum and daily ice cream van in summer.

What's On

Village events & festivals

Aug

RNLI Lifeboat Weekend

The Staithes and Runswick RNLI team hold a fundraising weekend with activities for all ages. Highlights include the traditional Nightgown Parade through the cobbled streets.

Festival of Arts & Heritage

Pop-up galleries in local cottages, guided walks, workshops, talks and film shows through the village's maze of lanes. The history of Staithes is celebrated with exhibitions throughout the weekend.

Captain Cook Heritage Centre

The village museum celebrates James Cook's early years in Staithes, where he worked as a grocer's apprentice before his life at sea. A must for history lovers of all ages.

Further Afield

Day trips from the cottage

Whitby

Historic whaling port, gothic abbey ruins, and the best fish and chips on the Yorkshire coast. An easy drive south.

Saltburn-by-the-Sea

Victorian seaside town with a famous pier, the UK's oldest water-powered cliff lift, and a surf school on the beach.

North York Moors

One of England's finest National Parks, with moorland walks, the steam railway, and moorside villages, all a short drive inland.

Robin Hood's Bay

A picturesque smugglers' village tumbling down to a rocky beach — perfect for a coastal walk or afternoon tea.
